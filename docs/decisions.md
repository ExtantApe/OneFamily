# Decisions

Dated decisions for this repo, newest last. The fuller design plan is kept privately by the owner. Decisions that matter here are copied in without private details.

- 2026-11-10: created from the ops starter kit, area `genealogy`.
- 2026-11-09 (owner): owned by Captive Origins, part of the OneOrigin family, under PolyForm Noncommercial. TypeScript.
- 2026-11-09 (owner): **public from the start.** The owner's words: "it's fine if it's public", then "It can be public? I mentioned that". The leak check runs in CI from the first commit, and a first-public-push audit comes before the first push.
- 2026-11-09 (owner): free, Mac only, no servers. The user chooses where the library lives. Two backups by default, with a deliberate way to go down to one or none.
- 2026-11-10 (owner): implement everything technically possible without breaking any service's terms. Enforced by the terms register (ADR 0004).
- 2026-11-09 (owner): our own exporter is built and tested against a mock site. It stays off for Ancestry until there's a partnership.
- 2026-11-09 (owner): no law-enforcement use from day one. Sanctioned, verifiable investigative genetic genealogy labs and wildlife agencies are allowed. See `USE-POLICY.md`. The exact line is still open.
- 2026-11-09 (owner): raw DNA uploads to GEDmatch only, done by the tester themselves. The app checks the law-enforcement setting.
- 2026-11-09 (owner): a GitHub connector keeps the history of a user's research only. It doesn't count as a backup.
- 2026-11-10: OneFamily reads published genetic maps in PLINK `.map` format, but doesn't bundle one yet. The usual GRCh37 map, distributed with Beagle and derived from HapMap, has no license stated on its download page. HapMap dropped its click-wrap license in 2004, but whether that covers derived map files isn't confirmed. Until it is, comparisons use the approximate 1 cM per Mb rule and say so. Bundling or downloading a map waits on the owner (task K320, owner item N124).
- 2026-11-10 (owner): the Chrome extension is the tool the Mac app uses to collect. It stores nothing, and talks to the app only through Chrome native messaging, never a localhost port. It's locked down: the host manifest's `allowed_origins` lists only our extension, and the host checks the caller's origin. The extension's `externally_connectable` has an empty `ids` list and no `matches`. A developer build ID is trusted only from the app's settings, as a recorded decision. The command list is fixed and short. Requests started in the extension need the owner's OK in the app: allow once, allow for this session (one kit, ending when Chrome closes or after 1 hour), or deny. Grants carry an expiry, only the minimum is handed over, the app logs what it handed over, and an active session grant shows in the app with "End now". Match details go in the side panel, never into the website's page. No automated page visits on any real site: build and test only against a local mock site with its own synthetic terms entry, at human pace (one page at a time, 30–60 s randomized pauses, a daily cap, and a stop at any challenge, sign-in page or error). A toolbar button toggles privacy in the app. See ADR 0009.
- 2026-11-10: privacy stand-ins on the testing services' own pages, through the extension, aren't built yet. They'd need a content script that writes into the page, which ADR 0009 avoids, and real services are refused by the terms register anyway. They're revisited only if a partnership allows reading those pages.
- 2026-11-12 (owner): the OneOrigin brand has its own palette and five marks, OneFamily's included: sky blue `#3fb1e4`, with the one in deep teal `#1f6f6b` and aubergine lines. `brand/oneorigin/` is a locked copy of OnePhylogeny's `docs/oneorigin/`, checked in CI. OneFamily's app icon, tile and maker's mark aren't decided yet.
- 2026-11-11 (owner, N101 B): OneFamily may import a match list that a user exports or saves themselves, for their own personal research. It's recorded as a deliberate reading of the services' terms, pending a legal review. The terms register has a separate `import_own_export` capability for this, allowed for the DNA services; the importers check it. Automated collection stays governed by `automate_page_visits`, which is still not allowed anywhere. Applied 2026-11-15.
- 2026-11-11 (owner, N102, all as recommended): identifying unknown remains through an accredited lab or a nonprofit is allowed. Identifying suspects is ruled out, even through a lab. Wildlife agencies are allowed for animal and wildlife cases only. Whether the policy covers the other OneOrigin apps goes to their maintainers first. In `USE-POLICY.md`.
- 2026-11-11 (owner, N106 A): draw the proposed taupe mark. Replaced on 2026-11-14 by D174: this app keeps the spiral mark, recolored, and the taupe proposal is dropped.
- 2026-11-11 (owner, N107 B): the legal review of the services' terms comes later. It's still needed before any partnership approach.
- 2026-11-11 (owner, N108): the private research folder stays where it is for now; Advanced Data Protection for iCloud is to be looked at when there's time. This concerns the owner's own files, not the app.
- 2026-11-11 (owner, N109 A): if labels are ever written back to Ancestry, they use free group slots only and never replace the user's own groups. Writing labels is still not allowed by Ancestry's terms, so nothing is built.
- 2026-11-11 (owner, N110 A): the owner contacts partner companies; Claude drafts. WikiTree and FamilySearch come first, the DNA companies after a working app, and only after the legal review.
- 2026-11-11 (owner, N124 A): the HapMap-derived GRCh37 map isn't bundled. The app will offer to download it from its source onto the user's own Mac, naming the source. Until that's built, results use the rough rule and say so.
- 2026-11-11 (owner, N126 B): anyone may partly rebuild the DNA of a relative who never tested, on real data too, once they've chosen to and the choice is recorded in their decisions log. The result is always labelled "rebuilt, partial", stays in their own library, and is never uploaded or shared by the app. Enforced by `rebuildFromDescendants`.
- 2026-11-11 (owner, N131 A, B, A): no Chrome Web Store listing yet; only a developer build trusted on the app's Extension screen can connect. The extension's toolbar button turns privacy on and off, as built. The collecting cap stays at 100 pages a day, as built.
- 2026-11-15 (Genealogy session, K338): OneFamily for Mac ships the official Node build inside the app, checked against a pinned SHA-256, instead of asking people to install Node. About 45 MB more in the download. See ADR 0007.
- 2026-11-15 (Genealogy session, K653, applying N124 A): the Kits screen offers the GRCh37 map from the Beagle genetic maps page at the University of Washington. It names the source, size and missing license before asking, downloads only after a yes, checks the archive against a pinned size and SHA-256, and keeps chromosomes 1 to 22 and X in the app's own folder with a note saying where they came from. It's used only when both raw files are on GRCh37; the demo keeps the approximate rule.
- 2026-11-25 (Software session, S3): the export inventory (`docs/export-coverage.md`) is clean room and says where its facts come from. It rests on our own simulator and importer, because the program's public documentation couldn't be found. K132 stays open until the owner names it.
- 2026-11-25 (Software session, S3): the backup-key recovery copy is shown on screen only, on request, and never goes to the library, a log or the clipboard. It's offered when a destination is cloud-synced.
- 2026-11-25 (Software session, S3): the app's mark is read from the locked brand copy through the `@brand` alias, so there is one source. `LICENSE` is unchanged; the longer restriction wording is a draft in `docs/license-restriction-draft.md` for the owner and a legal review.
